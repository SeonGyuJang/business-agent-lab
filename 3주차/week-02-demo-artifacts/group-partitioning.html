From preferences to groups

Business Agent DesignWeek 02
Fall 2026

State the decision before solving it

The model assigns every student to one group. It keeps students with request connections separate from truly isolated students, then rewards requests kept inside a group.

Sets and indices

S
The set of 45 students. Indices i and j identify students.
C
The 34 students with at least one incoming or outgoing request.
I
The 11 truly isolated students with no incoming or outgoing request. The sets C and I partition S.
G_K
The set of group labels {1, ..., K}. Index g identifies a group.
R
The set of 56 directed requests. Pair (i, j) belongs to R when student i named student j.

Parameters

K
The chosen number of groups. For 45 students and groups of size 2 to 4, feasible values run from 12 to 22.
L = 2
The minimum number of students allowed in a group.
U = 4
The maximum number of students allowed in a group.

Decision variables

x_ig = 1 if student i is assigned to group g; 0 otherwise.
y_ijg = 1 if students i and j are both assigned to group g; 0 otherwise.
z_g = 1 if group g is reserved for isolated students; 0 otherwise.

The variable yijg exists only for a directed request (i, j) in R.

Assign each student once

Sum over g in G_K of x_ig equals 1 for every i in S.

Every student belongs to exactly one group.

Keep group sizes valid

L is at most the sum over i in S of x_ig, which is at most U, for every g in G_K.

Each group contains at least 2 and at most 4 students.

Do not mix student types

Isolated students may enter only isolated-student groups. Connected students may enter only the other groups.

A group may contain students from the preference network or truly isolated students, but not both.

Link requests to assignments

Three inequalities link y_ijg to x_ig and x_jg.

These three inequalities make yijg equal 1 exactly when both students belong to group g.

Objective

Maximize the sum of y_ijg over all requests and groups.

Maximize the number of directed requests kept inside a group.
